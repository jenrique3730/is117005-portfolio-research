# New and emerging AI jobs: NY / NJ field scan

**Research checked:** November 5, 2026 · **Geography:** New York City and New Jersey, with nearby or NYC-commutable roles clearly labeled.  
Job postings change quickly. The examples below were reachable when checked; confirm the employer page before applying. This is a documented sample, not a count of all openings or a ranking of job growth.

## What the evidence says about growth

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects 2024–34 employment growth of **33.5% for data scientists**, **28.5% for information security analysts**, **21.5% for operations research analysts**, **19.7% for computer and information research scientists**, and **15.8% for software developers**. These are established occupational categories across the U.S. economy. BLS does not yet report a separate count for titles such as *forward-deployed AI engineer*, *LLM operations engineer*, or *AI red-team engineer*. Treat the BLS figures as evidence for adjacent skill families, not as a forecast for each new title. [BLS, July 16, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/artificial-intelligence-information-technology-and-employment-2024-34.htm)

For the region, the [New York State Department of Labor’s AI skills brief](https://dol.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/03/artificial-intelligence-ai-skill-in-new-york-state.pdf) reports that the New York–Newark–Jersey City metro led New York State in unique AI-related job postings in the Lightcast data it analyzed. It lists software developers, IT project managers, data scientists, and database architects among occupations with AI skill postings. NYC’s [2025 Local Workforce Plan](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/wkdev/downloads/pdf/New-York-City-2025-Local-Plan-and-Attachments-Signed-FINAL.pdf) reports more than 25,000 AI-related postings in 2024 and rising postings for AI engineer, cybersecurity consultant, and computer engineer titles from June 2024 to June 2025. These are useful regional signals, but the measures and periods differ from BLS’s national occupational projections.

### Coverage gaps in this sample

The postings we found give local evidence at internship and associate level for applied AI delivery, security automation, platform engineering, and customer engineering, plus mid-to-senior roles in platform engineering, forward deployment, model governance, and AI security. We found an AI/cybersecurity operations internship but fewer permanent roles explicitly labeled **junior AI security**, **junior AI operations**, or **AI data-center operations**. That is a gap in this sample, not evidence that such jobs do not exist. Many employers hire early-career candidates under established titles such as software engineer, security analyst, cloud support engineer, data engineer, and technical analyst, then assign them AI-related work.

## Emerging title families and what the jobs actually do

### Applied deployment and customer engineering

- **Forward-deployed engineer (FDE) / forward-deployed AI engineer:** Works directly with a customer or business team to understand a workflow, connect data and systems, build a production solution, and iterate based on use. Usually blends software engineering, integration, product judgment, and communication. FDE is a new label for work that overlaps with solutions engineering, implementation engineering, and technical consulting; postings vary widely in coding depth.
- **AI solutions engineer / AI deployment engineer:** Designs prototypes and integrations around a vendor’s model or platform, then helps customers deploy them. Compare quota or presales expectations with hands-on implementation in each posting.
- **AI transformation engineer / automation engineer:** Finds repetitive or document-heavy processes, builds AI-assisted workflows, and measures adoption, quality, and savings. Often sits between IT, operations, and business units.

### AI systems, infrastructure, and IT operations

- **AI platform engineer / ML platform engineer:** Builds shared services for model access, deployment, data pipelines, permissions, monitoring, and developer workflows.
- **LLM operations (LLMOps) / MLOps engineer:** Automates model and prompt versioning, evaluation, release, monitoring, rollback, and cost tracking. Titles often overlap with ML platform and DevOps roles.
- **Inference / AI infrastructure engineer:** Makes models run reliably and affordably in production; may work on GPU scheduling, serving, latency, model gateways, capacity, and cloud or on-prem infrastructure.
- **AI site reliability engineer (AI SRE):** Applies SRE methods to AI products, adding service objectives and incident response while also monitoring model quality, tool use, retrieval, and upstream model availability.
- **AI systems administrator / AI systems engineer:** Integrates approved AI services with identity, endpoints, networks, data access, logging, and enterprise applications. This work may be advertised under cloud, systems, or enterprise applications titles instead of “AI.”

### Security, safety, and assurance

- **AI security engineer / AI red-team engineer:** Tests model and agent systems for prompt injection, data exposure, unsafe tool execution, abuse, and insecure integrations; builds controls and repeatable evaluations.
- **AI security operations / AI threat analyst:** Uses security tooling and threat intelligence to investigate AI-enabled attacks and protect AI infrastructure. Sometimes an existing SOC or threat-intelligence job adds AI systems to its scope.
- **AI governance / model risk analyst:** Inventories AI use, assesses risk and controls, documents validation, tracks approvals, and monitors changes. Particularly visible in regulated New York financial services.
- **Model evaluator / AI quality engineer:** Creates test sets and evaluation harnesses, checks model or agent outputs against quality and safety criteria, triages failures, and verifies fixes before release.
- **AI safety / trust and safety operations specialist:** Reviews high-risk behavior, escalations, abuse patterns, and policy adherence; feeds incidents back to product, policy, and engineering teams.

### Data, product, and business operations

- **AI data quality / data operations specialist:** Checks source data, labels, permissions, freshness, and lineage that affect model and retrieval quality. Look for this work inside data steward, data quality, annotation operations, or analytics roles too.
- **AI product manager / agent product manager:** Defines the user problem, workflow, success metrics, and evaluation plan; coordinates engineering, design, security, legal, and operations.
- **AI operations analyst / AI program manager:** Coordinates adoption, vendors, controls, training, workflow changes, and performance measures across an organization. The title is inconsistent; responsibilities are the better search term.
- **AI infrastructure / data-center operations technician:** Maintains the power, cooling, networking, hardware, and incident procedures behind compute. This is an AI-adjacent infrastructure path; the work is usually found under data-center or critical-facilities titles, not “AI technician.”

## Entry routes and level signals

| Stage | Common signals in job descriptions | Practical entry evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internship / co-op | Current student, graduation window, mentor-led project, basic programming or systems coursework | A small deployed AI workflow, documented test results, a security lab, data pipeline, or automation project; explain what you built and how you checked it. |
| Entry / associate | 0–2 years, foundational coding/cloud/security skills, supervised ownership, support or implementation tasks | Internship, help-desk/cloud support, QA, SOC, data analyst, or software project experience that shows troubleshooting and clear documentation. |
| Mid-level | Independent delivery, production ownership, cross-team work, often 2–5+ years | Demonstrate a system used by others, measurable reliability or quality, incident handling, customer integration, or governance work. |
| Senior / staff / lead | Architecture, technical direction, risk decisions, mentoring, on-call or program ownership; years vary | Evidence of systems operated at scale, trade-offs owned, incident or audit outcomes, and influence across teams. |

A posting’s own requirements override this guide. Titles such as *engineer*, *analyst*, *architect*, and *AVP* do not indicate consistent seniority across employers.
