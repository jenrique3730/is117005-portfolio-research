# Role profiles: what the titles mean in practice

**Reviewed:** November 5, 2026 · **Evidence base:** NY/NJ employer postings and official labor-market sources linked in the [field scan](/research/jobs).

The “new AI job” market combines genuinely specialized work with established engineering, security, operations, product, and risk work applied to AI systems. The same duties often appear under several titles, so compare responsibilities and required experience rather than relying on a title alone.

## 1. Forward-deployed and AI deployment engineering

**Titles in postings:** Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), Forward Deployed AI Engineer, Forward Deployed Engineering Intern, Associate Field Engineer, AI Transformation Engineer.

**What the work involves:** Discover how a customer or internal team works; identify a valuable workflow; connect models to data, APIs, and enterprise systems; build a prototype; then harden, deploy, and improve it with the users. The engineer may return customer needs to the product team. It blends backend/software engineering, systems integration, problem discovery, and communication.

**Observed local range:** Google and Datasite posted NYC-area FDE internships; New York Life and Pinecone posted associate or entry-level AI/field engineering roles; Tavily’s NYC FDE asks for 3+ years; Citi has an AVP FDE role in New York. The title can therefore span student roles through experienced individual contributors, but postings vary sharply in experience and in-office expectations.

**Useful foundations:** Python or another backend language, APIs, SQL and data handling, cloud basics, retrieval and agent workflows, software testing, and the ability to explain trade-offs to users.

## 2. AI platform, infrastructure, and MLOps / LLMOps

**Titles in postings:** AI Platform Engineer, ML Platform Engineer, Platform Engineer – Enterprise AI, MLOps Engineer, LLMOps Engineer, Inference Infrastructure Engineer, AI Infrastructure Engineer.

**What the work involves:** Build and operate shared foundations that let teams run AI in production: model gateways, deployment workflows, tenant and data boundaries, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, model serving, observability, latency and cost controls, and incident processes. Some jobs include GPU scheduling or high-throughput inference; others focus on enterprise cloud services.

**Observed local evidence:** Turing’s NYC Enterprise AI Platform Engineer posting covers platform services, a model gateway, multi-tenant infrastructure, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, observability, on-call, and incident response, and asks for 6+ years. BMS’s Princeton Associate Engineer, Analytical AI Engineering posting offers an earlier-career entry into data pipelines, ML workflows, and platform features.

**Useful foundations:** Linux, networking, cloud infrastructure, containers, Python, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, monitoring, data pipelines, reliability, and security fundamentals. MLOps/LLMOps is often an extension of software, cloud, or data engineering rather than a separate entry-level track.

## 3. AI reliability and live operations

**Titles in postings:** AI Site Reliability Engineer, AI Live Operations Engineer, AI Reliability Engineer, Agent Reliability Engineer, LLM Operations Engineer.

**What the work involves:** Keep production AI services healthy after deployment. Responsibilities can include SLOs and incident response, model and agent monitoring, drift and quality signals, token and inference cost, fallback routing, continuous evaluation, release pipelines, and coordination with data science and platform teams.

**Observed local evidence:** Verizon’s Basking Ridge **Senior AI/ML Engineer – AI Live Operations** posting gives a detailed version of this work: monitor latency and token consumption, detect drift, run continuous evaluation, apply safety filters, use fallback systems, automate releases, and handle production incidents. It asks for 4+ years of experience. Schonfeld’s NYC SRE posting describes reliability ownership for an internal agentic AI platform, including SLOs, error budgets, observability, and incident response.

**Useful foundations:** SRE/DevOps or production software experience, monitoring and incident practice, distributed systems, deployment automation, and enough ML / LLM knowledge to interpret quality and model behavior signals.

## 4. AI security, cyber operations, and red teaming

**Titles in postings:** AI Security Engineer, Security Engineer – AI Platforms & Infrastructure, AI Red Team Engineer, AI Security Evaluator, Cybersecurity Operations & AI Automation Intern.

**What the work involves:** Secure models, agent workflows, tools, data, and infrastructure. Work may include threat modeling prompt injection and unsafe tool use, building secure evaluation environments, red-team testing, vulnerability management, security automation, access controls, monitoring, and response to AI-enabled threats.

**Observed local range:** Insmed’s Bridgewater 2027 internship pairs cybersecurity operations with AI automation and MCP connectors to security tools such as scanners and SIEMs. 10a Labs lists NYC among its target metros for a cybersecurity engineer building AI security evaluation infrastructure and security automation. Carlyle lists a Security Engineer – AI Platforms & Infrastructure role with New York as a location. These examples show internships and specialist engineering work; they do not establish a common AI-security career ladder.

**Useful foundations:** Networking and operating systems, secure software/cloud, IAM, logging and incident response, scripting, threat modeling, and standard cybersecurity practice. Add AI-specific testing such as prompt injection, data leakage, tool permissions, and model behavior evaluation.

## 5. AI governance, model risk, and assurance

**Titles in postings:** AI Governance Analyst / Associate / Specialist, Model Identification and Assurance Review Analyst, AI Risk and Controls Analyst, Model Risk Analyst, AI Governance Process Lead.

**What the work involves:** Track AI use cases and assets, classify risks, document controls, validate evidence, coordinate approval and oversight, monitor changes, and prepare for audit or regulator review. Some roles are technical and build test or monitoring frameworks; others emphasize policy, process, risk, and stakeholder coordination.

**Observed local range:** PwC posted an NYC AI Governance Experienced Associate; State Street posted a New York AI Governance Specialist, Officer; Citi posted model identification and assurance review work in New York State; Cognizant posted an AI Governance Process Lead in Burlington, NJ; and BMW Financial Services in Woodcliff Lake combines risk work with AI-enabled workflow modernization. The Citi role explicitly asks for 2–5 years in adjacent risk, governance, controls, or financial services experience, while BMW asks for 3–5 years. Entry can come from risk, audit, compliance, data, IT controls, or financial operations—not only from ML engineering.

**Useful foundations:** Risk assessment, process and control documentation, data governance, auditability, clear writing, stakeholder communication, and familiarity with AI/model lifecycles. Technical roles add SQL, Python, evaluation, monitoring, and cloud concepts.

## 6. Applied AI product and operations

**Titles in postings:** AI Product Manager Intern, Enterprise AI Intern, AI Transformation Engineer, AI Solutions Analyst, AI Program / Operations Analyst, Agent Product Manager.

**What the work involves:** Choose a user problem, define outcome metrics, coordinate implementation, test the workflow, plan controls and change management, and measure adoption and results. These roles often work across IT, operations, product, legal, security, and business teams.

**Observed local range:** SAP lists an NYC AI Product Management internship; Schonfeld’s 2027 Enterprise AI Intern owns a use-case backlog from requirements through rollout and adoption tracking; BMW’s Woodcliff Lake Risk Management and AI Solutions Analyst applies agents and workflow automation to existing risk processes.

**Useful foundations:** Product or business analysis, workflow mapping, data literacy, requirements and testing, AI limitations, security/privacy awareness, and the ability to define measurable outcomes.

## 7. AI data engineering and analytical systems

**Titles in postings:** AI Data Engineer, Data Quality / AI Data Operations Specialist, Associate Engineer – Analytical AI Engineering, Data Scientist, Analytics Engineer.

**What the work involves:** Prepare governed, reliable data for AI; build ingestion and transformation pipelines; maintain data quality and lineage; and support model or agent workflows. In many companies this is still advertised under established data engineering or analytics titles, even when the work directly enables AI.

**Observed local evidence:** BMS’s Princeton associate role describes data pipelines and ML workflow components. NYS Department of Labor’s AI skills report lists software developers, IT project managers, data scientists, and database architects among occupations appearing in AI-skilled postings. BLS separately projects strong national growth for data scientists; that projection is for the occupation, not a distinct AI data engineer title.

**Useful foundations:** SQL, Python, databases, ETL/ELT, data quality, cloud data platforms, access controls, documentation, and an understanding of how retrieval and model inputs affect output quality.

## Career movement: common adjacent paths

- **Cloud support / systems administration → cloud or platform engineering → AI platform / inference operations.** Add scripting, CI/CD, observability, containers, and safe model-serving knowledge.
- **SOC analyst / security analyst → security engineering → AI security evaluation or AI platform security.** Add Python, cloud identity and logging, agent permissions, prompt-injection testing, and adversarial evaluation.
- **Data analyst / analytics engineer → data engineering or data science → AI data or model operations.** Build stronger software engineering, production pipeline, and evaluation skills.
- **Business analyst / risk or audit analyst → AI governance / model assurance → governance program leadership.** Add AI lifecycle literacy, technical controls, and evidence-based risk evaluation.
- **Software engineer / solutions engineer → associate field engineer → forward-deployed AI engineer.** Build customer discovery, integration, retrieval, agent testing, and deployment experience.
- **Product / operations analyst → AI product or AI transformation → enterprise AI program leadership.** Learn workflow design, metrics, integration constraints, privacy, and rollout practice.

These are reasonable pathways inferred from the overlapping responsibilities and qualifications in the cited postings; they are not guaranteed employer promotion ladders.
