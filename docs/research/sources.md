# Sources and research method

**Last checked:** November 5, 2026. Employer listings can close, change titles, or alter qualifications after this date. Confirm the source page before using a posting to make an application decision.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Official labor data and government workforce reports** provide occupation definitions, long-term projections, or regional context. These sources use different geographies and measures; do not combine their values into one growth rate.
2. **Employer career pages** show the actual title, location, responsibilities, work arrangement, experience requirements, and (when posted) application dates or pay ranges. They are evidence that an employer advertised one role, not proof of overall demand.
3. **Job aggregators** can help discover a role but may lag, omit context, or keep expired listings. The field scan labels those entries and recommends checking the employer site.

## Official labor and regional sources

- [BLS: Artificial intelligence, information technology, and employment, 2024–34](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/artificial-intelligence-information-technology-and-employment-2024-34.htm) — U.S. occupational projections for related established job families. BLS does not publish separate projections for every recent AI-specific title.
- [BLS: Occupational projections and worker characteristics](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/occupational-projections-and-characteristics.htm) — occupation-level projected employment, education, experience, and wage information.
- [BLS: Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/) — work activities, typical entry education, pay, and outlook by occupation.
- [New York State Department of Labor: Artificial Intelligence (AI) Skill in New York State](https://dol.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/03/artificial-intelligence-ai-skill-in-new-york-state.pdf) — Lightcast posting and skill context; identifies New York–Newark–Jersey City as the leading New York State metro for unique AI-related postings in its analysis.
- [NYC 2025 Local Workforce Plan](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/wkdev/downloads/pdf/New-York-City-2025-Local-Plan-and-Attachments-Signed-FINAL.pdf) — city workforce analysis reporting AI posting counts and changes across selected titles and periods.
- [World Economic Forum: Future of Jobs Report 2025](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/) — survey of employer expectations, useful for context but not a count of NY/NJ job openings.

## Method

- Search for title variants across AI application and deployment, security, infrastructure, operations, data, product, and governance.
- Prioritize New York City and New Jersey postings; mark nearby or upstate locations so they are not mistaken for the NYC metro area.
- Capture internship, associate/early-career, mid-level, and senior examples when the employer posting supports that classification.
- Classify levels from employer language such as years of experience, student status, “associate,” or explicit seniority. Employer title ladders vary, so an AVP or “in training” label is not treated as universally comparable.
- Describe the duties in plain language and preserve the employer page link. Avoid copying full job descriptions.
- Separate the occupation outlook from the new-title evidence. BLS projects established occupations; job postings document specific employer demand at a point in time.
- Recheck every listing before applying. Search results and employer career pages are snapshots, and this portfolio is not an automatically refreshed job feed.

## Limitations

This is a curated sample of accessible postings, not an exhaustive census of NY/NJ hiring. Search indexing favors well-indexed employer systems and may miss small companies, staffing firms, closed listings, and roles advertised under traditional titles. “Fast-growing” is supported here by broad national occupational projections and regional workforce reports; it is not asserted for every new title. Availability, location, seniority, and pay may change.
